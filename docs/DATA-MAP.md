Written 13 November 2026 from `docs/BRIEF.md`, the spec, and the database as it exists today (changes 0001 to 0007). The technical name is in brackets so the reviewers can match each line to the database. "Owner" is the part of the server that is allowed to write that thing (see `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, Part 2).

## 1. The picture in one page

- A **customer** (today only Cosmos) is one plant. Everything below belongs to exactly one customer, and the database itself refuses to show one customer's rows to another.
- A plant has **people** and **logins**. A person is a human being (a technician, an operator, a vendor's engineer). A login is a username and password; it is either personal (tied to one person) or a shared phone on the floor. On a shared phone, each action also records which person did it.
- A plant has **machines**, sorted into segments, groups and cells, and a set of **lists and settings** that make it this plant and no other (wait reasons, work types, shifts, targets, wording).
- A **ticket** is one breakdown or one work request. Everything that happens to it is written as a line in its **history**, in order, and never edited afterwards. The history is the truth; the numbers on the ticket (response time, downtime, hands-on time) are worked out from it.
- When work happens, the ticket records **who was on the job** and **each stretch of time each person worked**. When it is closed, a **breakdown report** is filled in and a PDF is made.
- From M2, each ticket has **two clocks** (acknowledge and restore), a **ladder** of who is told when a clock is missed, a **duty roster** saying who is on that ladder today, and a **sign-off** by production.
- From M4, the old breakdown history and the live tickets feed the **analytics engine**, which stores its results (KPIs, forecasts, the monthly scorecard). **Preventive maintenance** plans create scheduled tasks. Each closed breakdown gets **failure codes**.
- From M6, the **AI** stores its suggestions and whether a person accepted them. A suggestion changes nothing until a person confirms it.
- Last, in M3, **WhatsApp** contacts, conversations, messages and voice notes.

## 3. How the things connect

- Customer → people, logins, machines, lists, settings, tickets. Nothing is shared between customers.
- Login → at most one person. A shared login has no person of its own.
- Machine → one segment, one group, one cell at a time; its moves are dated.
- Ticket → at most one machine (none for general work), many history lines, many crew members, many work stretches, at most one breakdown report, any number of follow-up tasks (each a small ticket pointing back to its parent).
- Ticket → its clocks, its escalation records, its sign-off (M2); its failure coding (M4b); its AI suggestions (M6); its WhatsApp conversation (M3).
- Machine → PM plans → scheduled tasks → completions (M4b). Machine → figures and forecasts (M4).
- Person appears on tickets, history, crew, work stretches, reports, roster, notifications. This is why a person is switched off rather than deleted.

## 5. Personal data and the law (DPDP Act 2023)

What we hold about people is small and work-related: name, job title, team, shift, optionally a mobile number, a username, what they did on tickets and when, and later their voice and WhatsApp messages. No home address, no date of birth, no ID numbers, no health data, no data about children. It stays that way.

- **Only what is needed.** A mobile number is optional and is needed only for WhatsApp (M3). Operators who only use a shared phone need no login and no number, only a name on the pick-list.
- **Purpose.** Data is used to run and improve maintenance at that plant. Any other use needs the customer's agreement (question 7).
- **Location.** Everything is stored in AWS Mumbai. Nothing leaves India unless a written decision with a data-protection note says so (BRIEF). Push notifications pass through Google's and Apple's services, as with any app; their content is kept to the ticket number and machine, with no names.
- **Consent.** A consent message on first WhatsApp contact; voice notes only from people who agreed (need N6, N14).
- **Our role.** The plant decides what is collected; we process it for them under a short agreement.
- **Deletion and correction.** A person can be corrected or switched off at any time. Full removal conflicts with keeping the ticket record intact; question 1 asks how you want that handled.
- **No real data in the code.** Tests and screenshots use made-up names. Real Cosmos data on the staging site needs your answer to question 3.
- **Logs** carry ids, never names, numbers or message text.

## 6. Changes to data that already exists

Nothing in this plan alters or deletes data that is already stored. Every new thing above is a new table added beside the old ones. Two existing things gain rules, not changes: the repeat guard starts being cleaned after 30 days, and import rows have their original content cleared a year after acceptance. Both wait for your answers below and for the schema reviewer.

The standing rule for any later change that does touch stored data:

1. **Backup first.** A full database snapshot is taken and its id written into the change's plan before the change runs. On staging first, always.
2. **Add, then switch, then remove.** A change first adds the new shape alongside the old, the app is released using the new shape, and only a later release removes the old one. Nothing is destroyed in the same release that introduces its replacement.
3. **Roll back.** Because the old shape is still there, going back is re-releasing the previous version. If data was changed wrongly, the snapshot from step 1 is restored; the restore is rehearsed before cutover and every quarter.
4. **Checked.** The schema reviewer must pass it, and the automatic check refuses a destructive change that has no approval.

## Questions for Nimish

Product and business only. Where I propose a default, saying "yes" is enough.

1. **When someone leaves the plant.** Old tickets name them, and those tickets are the accountability record. Proposed: keep the name on past tickets, switch the person off, delete their mobile number, logins, devices and WhatsApp contact. If the person or Cosmos later asks for full removal, replace the name with "Former staff" everywhere. Agree?
2. **Mobile numbers.** Collect them only when WhatsApp starts (M3), and only for people who will use it? Or does Cosmos want numbers on file from M1 for its own use?
3. **Real Cosmos data on the staging site** (N14). May the test site hold the real machines, people and tickets, or must it use made-up people? This decides how the import rehearsal is run.
4. **Individual performance.** Today a technician sees only their own minutes; supervisors, the head and admin see anyone's; the plant head sees team totals. Should analytics ever rank or compare named technicians (minutes, repeat faults, response time), or stay at team and machine level? This is a labour-relations question at Cosmos as much as a product one.
5. **Money.** Cost and turnover figures are shown to the maintenance head, plant head and admin only. Correct?
6. **How long to keep things.** Proposed: tickets, reports, figures and forecasts forever; audit trail 3 years; notifications 90 days; WhatsApp messages 1 year; voice recordings 90 days after the text is confirmed; AI suggestions 2 years; duty roster 1 year. Any you want longer or shorter?
7. **Using a plant's data beyond that plant.** May a customer's tickets be used (a) to test the AI for that same customer, (b) to improve the product for other customers, with names removed? Proposed: (a) yes, (b) only with written agreement in the customer contract.
8. **AI and India.** May ticket text be sent to an AI service outside India if names and numbers are removed first and Cosmos agrees in writing, or must AI run in India only? "India only" narrows the choice of AI and may raise the monthly cost; I will show the trade-off when M6 is sized.
9. **If a customer leaves.** Proposed: they get a full export (tickets, reports, PDFs) on request, and everything is deleted 90 days after the contract ends. Agree?
10. **Our own access.** Who at Cosmos may approve our support access to their data, and for how long at a time? Proposed: the admin approves; 7 days at most; every action visible to them in the audit trail.
11. **Gujarati wording.** The Gujarati labels Cosmos's team writes: are they Cosmos's alone, or may they become the starting wording for the next Gujarati-speaking plant?
12. **Tags.** Tickets can carry free tags from a list. The roadmap notes they overlap with severity and ticket state. Keep tags as a plant-defined label, or drop them before cutover?
13. **Manuals.** Machine makers' manuals are their copyright. Proposed: a plant uploads its own manuals, they are visible to that plant only, and are never shared across customers. Agree?
14. **Operators who sign off a fix (M2).** May any operator in the same cell confirm or dispute, or only the person who raised the ticket? The spec says "same cell"; confirm that suits Cosmos.

Not for now: the escalation questions (who is on the ladder, delays, repeats, who may acknowledge, what the notification says) wait until two weeks after cutover, as the BRIEF says; and which of Bhavin's analytics screens Cosmos really uses is settled in the triage with Bhavin before M4a.
