- A plant's numbers and the order of its tables no longer depend on the order its same-day records arrive in. The server sends the records of one day sorted by their database id, and two plants holding the same records under different ids printed different last decimals (a monthly-report chart value of 18 against 17.9), different "longest breakdowns" rows in Compare, a different order in Why-Why, the Breakdown Log, the Data Checklist and the monthly "Where the time went" table, and a different machine at the top of the repeat-fault table. The engine now puts the records of each day in one order made of business values (the log number, the shift, the machine's place in the plant's list, the ticket reference) before it computes anything, so every computer reads the same plant the same way.
- The breakdown and cell-move sheets of the Excel export list same-day rows by the same business order instead of by id.
- A new test runs every figure over three plants loaded the way the server loads them under two different sets of ids, and with each day's records shuffled, and requires identical figures.
